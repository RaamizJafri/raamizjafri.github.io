The Politics of Pilgrimage

Hajj Quota Allocations and Muslim Population Rates Across the Middle East

GEOG 395 Cartography and Geovisualization · Texas A&M University · Spring 2026

The problem

Saudi Arabia controls how many pilgrims each country may send to Hajj through a quota of one slot per 1,000 Muslim residents. The project asked which Middle Eastern countries send the most pilgrims to Mecca and whether that number reflects the quota.

My role

Individual final project: I designed the maps, built the chart, and assembled and revised the StoryMap.

Data and method

Data

  • World Population Review, Hajj quota by country
  • Pew Research Center, Muslim population change (2025)
  • Statista, Saudi Arabia total Hajj pilgrims
  • Saudi General Authority for Statistics, annual Hajj attendance, 2016–2023
  • Iran International (2024)

Method

  • Designed a bivariate thematic map in ArcGIS Pro comparing two variables across 10 Middle Eastern countries: absolute Hajj quota and quota as a rate of Muslim population
  • Used a 3x3 bivariate color matrix (teal for quota, amber/orange for rate), classified each variable with Natural Breaks (Jenks), and assigned hex colors manually because ArcGIS Pro has no built-in bivariate choropleth tool
  • Resolved ArcGIS Pro publishing errors (projection conflict 00230, in-memory join 00226, symbol class visibility 00422) and published the map to ArcGIS Online
  • Built a flow map where line thickness is proportional to each country's quota
  • Built a bar chart of annual Hajj attendance, 2016–2023
  • Assembled everything into an ArcGIS StoryMap, collected feedback from three reviewers, and revised the StoryMap in response

Map

Quota categories by country. Click a point to see its category. Points sit at approximate country centers; the lines toward Mecca echo the flow map.

What I found

  • Bahrain had the highest rate: 2,094 slots for about 866,000 Muslims, about 0.24%
  • Iran had the largest absolute quota at 87,550 slots, about 0.10% of its roughly 84 million Muslims
  • Turkey and Egypt fell into Medium Quota / Low Rate; Iraq and Jordan into Low Quota / Low Rate
  • Attendance fell from about 2.49 million (2019) to 10,000 (2020) and 60,000 (2021) under COVID-19 restrictions, then recovered to about 1.85 million by 2023

Overall the map reveals that Hajj quota allocation is not a straightforward reflection of Muslim population size.

What I would do differently

The overlapping flow lines at Mecca made the flow map hard to read; I would add labels over each line to distinguish which country connects with which line.
