Obesity & Recreational Access

Exploring Spatial Patterns of Obesity & Recreational Access in the United States

30.6187° N · 96.3365° W

GEOG 478 · Texas A&M University · Team project · My role: data collection and dashboard design

Team: Geena Ginu, Adam Burns, Raamiz Jafri, Julian Gomez

Problem

Obesity rates vary across states, and access to recreational facilities may be one factor shaping these patterns. Research question: Do states with lower access to recreational facilities have higher obesity rates?

What we did

  • Collected state-level adult obesity rates from the CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), 2022
  • Collected recreational facility data from the EPA Smart Location Database and Homeland Infrastructure Foundation-Level Data (HIFLD)
  • Used U.S. Census Bureau 2022 state population estimates to normalize facilities per 100,000 residents
  • Joined and cleaned the datasets by state
  • Built an interactive WebGIS application with Leaflet.js, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and GeoJSON state boundaries
  • Created two choropleth layers (obesity rate; recreational facilities per 100,000 residents) with layer toggling and click-for-detail popups

What we found

  • A moderate negative correlation (r = -0.33): states with greater recreational access tend to have lower obesity rates
  • Southern states such as Mississippi, Alabama, and Louisiana show consistently higher obesity rates
  • Western and northern states generally show lower obesity rates and greater recreational access; Montana, Wyoming, and Alaska rank among the highest in recreational facility access per capita
  • The correlation is not strong enough to imply causation; income, education, diet, healthcare access, and cultural behaviors likely also play a role

What I would do differently

State-level data and missing factors like income and diet affect the results; I would add more variables and expand the analysis to city-level data.
