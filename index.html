The Politics of PilgrimageHajj Quota Allocations and Muslim Population Rates Across the Middle East · GEOG 395 Cartography and Geovisualization · Spring 2026

Problem

Saudi Arabia controls how many pilgrims each country may send to Hajj through a quota of one slot per 1,000 Muslim residents. The project asked which Middle Eastern countries send the most pilgrims to Mecca and whether that number reflects the quota.

What I did

  • Designed a bivariate thematic map in ArcGIS Pro comparing two variables across 10 Middle Eastern countries: absolute Hajj quota and quota as a rate of Muslim population
  • Used a 3x3 bivariate color matrix (teal for quota, amber/orange for rate), classified each variable with Natural Breaks (Jenks), and assigned hex colors manually because ArcGIS Pro has no built-in bivariate choropleth tool
  • Resolved ArcGIS Pro publishing errors (projection conflict 00230, in-memory join 00226, symbol class visibility 00422) and published the map to ArcGIS Online
  • Built a flow map where line thickness is proportional to each country's quota
  • Built a bar chart of annual Hajj attendance, 2016–2023 (Saudi General Authority for Statistics)
  • Assembled everything into an ArcGIS StoryMap, collected feedback from three reviewers, and revised the StoryMap in response

What I found

  • Bahrain had the highest rate: 2,094 slots for about 866,000 Muslims, about 0.24%
  • Iran had the largest absolute quota at 87,550 slots, about 0.10% of its roughly 84 million Muslims
  • Turkey and Egypt fell into Medium Quota / Low Rate; Iraq and Jordan into Low Quota / Low Rate
  • Attendance fell from about 2.49 million (2019) to 10,000 (2020) and 60,000 (2021) under COVID-19 restrictions, then recovered to about 1.85 million by 2023

Overall the map reveals that Hajj quota allocation is not a straightforward reflection of Muslim population size.

Click a point to see its category. Points sit at approximate country centers; the line style toward Mecca echoes the flow map.

What I would do differently

The overlapping flow lines at Mecca made the flow map hard to read; I would add labels over each line to distinguish which country connects with which line.

Exploring Spatial Patterns of Obesity & Recreational Access in the United StatesGEOG 478 · Texas A&M University · Team project · My role: data collection and dashboard design

Team: Geena Ginu, Adam Burns, Raamiz Jafri, Julian Gomez

Problem

Obesity rates vary across states, and access to recreational facilities may be one factor shaping these patterns. Research question: Do states with lower access to recreational facilities have higher obesity rates?

What we did

  • Collected state-level adult obesity rates from the CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), 2022
  • Collected recreational facility data from the EPA Smart Location Database and Homeland Infrastructure Foundation-Level Data (HIFLD)
  • Used U.S. Census Bureau 2022 state population estimates to normalize facilities per 100,000 residents
  • Joined and cleaned the datasets by state
  • Built an interactive WebGIS application with Leaflet.js, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and GeoJSON state boundaries
  • Created two choropleth layers (obesity rate; recreational facilities per 100,000 residents) with layer toggling and click-for-detail popups

What we found

  • A moderate negative correlation (r = -0.33): states with greater recreational access tend to have lower obesity rates
  • Southern states such as Mississippi, Alabama, and Louisiana show consistently higher obesity rates
  • Western and northern states generally show lower obesity rates and greater recreational access; Montana, Wyoming, and Alaska rank among the highest in recreational facility access per capita
  • The correlation is not strong enough to imply causation; income, education, diet, healthcare access, and cultural behaviors likely also play a role

What I would do differently

State-level data and missing factors like income and diet affect the results; I would add more variables and expand the analysis to city-level data.

Data: CDC BRFSS 2022 Adult Obesity Prevalence Maps; EPA Smart Location Database; HIFLD Public Recreational Areas; U.S. Census Bureau 2022 State Population Estimates; Ding et al. (2011); Sallis et al. (2016).
